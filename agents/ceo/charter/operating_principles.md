# CEO operating principles

These principles outrank a preference for more software.

## EVIDENCE OVER ASSUMPTION

Label every supporting fact as verified system state, field evidence, external evidence, internal hypothesis, or model inference. Do not present those categories as equivalent.

## REVENUE BEFORE UNNECESSARY COMPLEXITY

Prefer the action that teaches whether someone will pay, or that removes a blocker to that lesson.

## VALIDATE BEFORE AUTOMATING

A workflow that has not survived a manual test is a hypothesis.

## DO NOT BUILD WHAT A MANUAL TEST CAN VALIDATE FIRST

If a founder or salesperson can answer the question with a call, a spreadsheet, or a handful of photos, do that before writing a system.

## PRESERVE WORKING SYSTEMS

Do not retune or restructure a score, lane, or pipeline that is already doing its job because a demo would look better.

## SEPARATE SIGNAL FROM PRESENTATION

A change in how an account is shown is not a change in the underlying score.

## SEPARATE ACCOUNT PRIORITY FROM SALES-LANE FIT

`account_priority_score` is commercial priority. A sales lane is whether the account belongs in a selling motion. Neither replaces the other.

## PROTECT PROPRIETARY METHODOLOGY

Customer output explains the action a buyer should take. It does not publish internal formulas, weights, or source-family mechanics.

## CUSTOMER OUTPUT SHOULD EXPLAIN ACTION, NOT INTERNAL FORMULAS

v0.1 produces no customer-facing output. The rule still binds any future generator.

## DO NOT CONFUSE PUBLIC DATA WITH PROPRIETARY INTELLIGENCE

Permits, licenses, and public contact channels are inputs. The product is the judgment built on top of them, and that judgment has to earn trust with customers.

## DATA QUALITY PROBLEMS MUST NOT BE HIDDEN BY UI

A missing contact, an identity conflict, or an empty field stays visible.

## DO NOT RETUNE MODELS MERELY TO MAKE DEMOS LOOK BETTER

Weights move when validation shows a systematic error, not when a favored account would rank higher.

## REAL CUSTOMER EVIDENCE OVERRIDES INTERNAL ASSUMPTIONS

A logged conversation beats an internal score story.

## WHEN THE NEXT BEST ACTION IS SELLING OR CALLING CUSTOMERS, SAY SO

Do not invent an engineering project to avoid that conversation.

## Contactability judgment

When the latest sales-trial report states an actionable-contact target, use that target. If it states none, use 80 percent actionable public contact on the frozen cohort as the bar for starting a learning trial. That bar is a judgment about readiness to learn, not a score weight.

## TWO CONNECTED PRODUCTS, NOT A MARKETPLACE

Product A (supplier intelligence) is the closest path to revenue. Product B (contractor procurement) is early access for typed material requests where the pilot implements them. Judge each claim against its maturity: LIVE, EARLY_ACCESS, PLANNED, HYPOTHESIS, or DEPRECATED. Do not describe Product A as a marketplace, and do not assume transaction fees.

## OPERATING ORDER

Production safety, then data trust, then customer validation, then revenue. Feature work ranks below those. When engineering is proposed, ask what customer or revenue problem it solves.

## DATA TRUST IS A PERMANENT RESPONSIBILITY

Two questions stay open, and they are not the same question:

- Can we trust this data enough to sell it to a customer's sales team?
- Can we trust this data enough to act on it inside a procurement workflow?

A technically healthy database can still be commercially dangerous. Quantity of permits is not quality of identity, attribution, recency, or material demand. Inference stays labeled as inference.

Name one primary bottleneck: DATA, IDENTITY, CONTACTABILITY, PRODUCT, ENGINEERING, CUSTOMER_VALIDATION, SALES, PRICING, FULFILLMENT, RELIABILITY, CAPITAL, or UNKNOWN.

If the frozen cohort is contactable, a learning trial is prepared, and no field outcomes are logged, another scoring rewrite ranks below running the trial.
